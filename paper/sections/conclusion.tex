\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper characterized the post-training threshold-calibration stage in federated IoT anomaly detection as an attack surface the studied training- and aggregation-phase defenses do not address.
Under a score-level contamination model on the N-BaIoT nine-device federation (FedAvg autoencoder, $q{=}0.95$, single compromised client), the raising attack satisfied the pre-specified material threshold-displacement criterion in every evaluated non-zero policy--fraction condition, with 95\% bootstrap intervals on $\Delta\tau$ excluding zero, and degraded victim detection rates by 2.4--7.9\,pp, consistently signed in all ten seeds.
The lowering attack shifts thresholds downward, raising the FPR-disparity metric $\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)}$ by 0.05--0.06 for \LOCAL and \CLUSTER at $f{=}0.4$, a lower bound under Replace-Fixed-Budget; the accompanying $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ increase reflects operating-point displacement, not a security benefit.
The policy ordering---maximal exposure under \LOCAL, diluted but federation-wide threshold propagation under \GLOBAL, small intra-cluster perturbations under \CLUSTER---suggests that in settings resembling this one, calibration-stage hardening should be matched to the threshold policy rather than left to training-phase defenses.
These findings isolate a calibration-channel vulnerability mechanism, not a complete traffic-generation attack; traffic-level realizability, calibration-stage integrity defenses, and generalization across detectors and datasets remain open.

\textbf{Reproducibility.}
The artifact bundle archived on Zenodo at \doi{10.5281/zenodo.21196568} contains the configuration, paired seeds, split manifest, per-cell results, negative-control records, isolation checks, and figure/table scripts.
Checkpoints, hyperparameters, and analysis gates were fixed before poisoned runs; no poisoned metric informed any of those choices.
