\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Federated learning (FL) is commonly used for IoT anomaly detection: devices contribute to a shared model without centralizing raw traffic, and personalized detection thresholds let each node adapt to its own traffic distribution~\cite{rey2022federated}.
Security work on federated anomaly detection has concentrated on the \emph{training phase}: Byzantine clients injecting adversarial gradients, label-flipping poisoning, or model-inversion attacks~\cite{bhagoji2019analyzing,biggio2012poisoning,tolpegin2020data}.
Aggregation-phase defenses such as FLTrust, Krum, and Trimmed-Mean target exactly this surface~\cite{blanchard2017machine,cao2020fltrust,yin2018byzantine}.
Recent FL-IDS work adds personalized, non-IID-robust, knowledge-distillation, and anti-poisoning designs~\cite{Belenguer2025FLIDSReview,Khang2026P4P,Quyen2025FedKDIDS,Thein2024PFLIDS}, all targeting the training or aggregation surface rather than the post-training calibration buffer.

\textbf{A post-training coverage gap.}
The \emph{threshold-calibration stage} is the post-training, pre-deployment step in which each client collects a window of benign traffic, computes local reconstruction scores, and derives its detection threshold at a fixed quantile~$q$.
Training- and aggregation-phase defenses complete before calibration begins and cannot observe the calibration buffer.
If a single client's benign calibration buffer can be contaminated with benign scores drawn from the upper (or lower) tail of that client's own distribution, the resulting threshold shifts without any change to model weights, training data, labels, or gradients.
No claim is made that a dedicated calibration-stage defense is impossible, only that the training- and aggregation-phase defenses studied in the FL-IDS literature do not cover this stage.

\textbf{Study focus.}
This paper identifies threshold calibration as an underexplored attack surface and characterizes its vulnerability under a score-level contamination model.
Concretely, an adversary with access to a single client's calibration buffer replaces a fraction~$f$ of entries with tail-sampled scores drawn from the same client's benign distribution (\emph{calibration-stage poisoning}).
The evaluation covers three threshold-policy families---\GLOBAL (federation-wide mean), \LOCAL (per-client), and \CLUSTER (cluster-averaged)---across all nine physical-device clients in the N-BaIoT dataset~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} at four injection fractions.

Calibration-stage poisoning is not simply data poisoning relocated to calibration data (Table~\ref{tab:related}).
It occurs strictly after model convergence, so training-phase defenses have already completed; it exploits a trust model specific to that phase, since calibration data is collected autonomously with no central validation gate; and its effect depends on the threshold policy, so the same injection fraction produces different shifts and different propagation under \GLOBAL, \LOCAL, and \CLUSTER.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The analysis isolates the post-training threshold-calibration stage as a distinct attack surface: contaminating one client's benign calibration scores can shift thresholds while model weights, training data, aggregation, test scores, and labels remain fixed (\S\ref{sec:threat}).
  \item The evaluation presents a controlled vulnerability characterization under score-level calibration contamination across three threshold policies, showing that the raising attack degrades victim detection rates by 2.4--7.9\,pp across non-zero injection fractions (4.9--7.9\,pp at $f{=}0.4$) with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals excluding zero (\S\ref{sec:results}).
  \item The results document policy-differentiated behavior: \LOCAL gives the largest per-victim threshold shifts with no cross-client threshold propagation; \CLUSTER allows small intra-cluster threshold perturbations; \GLOBAL dilutes shifts but propagates them federation-wide; lowering is secondary and conditionally bounded, especially under \GLOBAL (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope.}
All claims are bounded to the nine-device N-BaIoT setting with autoencoder detectors and FedAvg aggregation, and the poisoning mechanism operates at the score level; no claim is made about deployability, evasion guarantees, or generalization beyond this setting.
Defense design against calibration-stage poisoning is out of scope, though \S\ref{sec:discussion} discusses properties a defense would need to satisfy.
