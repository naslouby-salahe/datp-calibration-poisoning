\subsection{Clean-Baseline Policy Profile}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} reports clean-baseline dispersion across all ten seeds.
Lower CV(FPR) means more uniform per-client false-positive treatment: \LOCAL is lowest ($0.333 \pm 0.034$), \GLOBAL highest ($0.905 \pm 0.039$), and \CLUSTER intermediate ($0.615 \pm 0.143$) with cluster-assignment sensitivity.
Pairwise CV(FPR) gaps are positive with bootstrap CIs excluding zero: G--L $0.572$ $[0.537, 0.607]$, G--C $0.291$ $[0.214, 0.358]$, and C--L $0.281$ $[0.201, 0.374]$ (unrounded per-seed aggregates); Fig.~\ref{fig:clean-fpr} shows the underlying per-client FPR distributions.
Lower dispersion does not imply uniformly better detection: \GLOBAL retains the best P10 Macro-F1---the 10th percentile of per-client macro-F1, a worst-case detection-quality metric---at $0.398$ against \LOCAL's $0.335$.
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} therefore reports dispersion, detection quality, and mean FPR/TPR rather than a single summary.

\subsection{Raising Attack: Primary Results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold-shift}(a) report threshold shift and downstream detection harm for High-Score-Benign + Threshold-Raise.

\input{tables/table_main_results}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_threshold_shift.pdf}
\caption{Mean threshold shift $\Delta\tau$ vs.\ injection fraction $f$ for raising (a) and lowering (b) attacks.
Error bars are 95\% bootstrap CIs over 10 seeds; \GLOBAL is diluted by $1/N$, and Random-Benign controls are near-null (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}).
Panel (b) uses the legend of panel (a).}
\label{fig:threshold-shift}
\end{figure}

\textbf{All three policies clear every gate.}
The seed-level Gate-1 pass rate is 100\% (10/10 seeds) at every non-zero fraction for all three policies.
For \LOCAL and \CLUSTER, all nine clients are individually material in every seed.
For \GLOBAL at $f{=}0.1$, two devices (Provision PT-737E and PT-838) are non-material in 2/10 and 4/10 seeds respectively, but the victim-majority condition ($\ge$5/9 per seed) still holds because the remaining seven clients are consistently material.
Bootstrap CIs on $\Delta\tau$ exclude zero and do not overlap the Random-Benign control.
For that control, CIs span zero, the Gate-1 pass rate is $0\%$, and $|\Delta\mathrm{TPR}|{\le}1$\,pp at every fraction and policy (Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports representative $f{=}0.4$ rows; the artifact contains the full near-null matrix).
The targeted effects are therefore attributable to tail contamination, not position perturbation alone.

\textbf{Policy magnitude.}
Raising-attack magnitude differs by policy.
\LOCAL and \CLUSTER exhibit the largest threshold shifts: at $f{=}0.4$, $\Delta\tau = +0.902$ $[+0.864, +0.944]$ and $+0.899$ $[+0.859, +0.941]$ respectively.
\GLOBAL shows a diluted but still material shift: $+0.100$ $[+0.096, +0.105]$ at $f{=}0.4$.
The dilution is expected, since \GLOBAL's threshold is the federation mean and a single-client shift is divided by $N{=}9$, but the residual magnitude ($\approx$10\% of the local shift) still produces measurable downstream harm.

\textbf{Downstream harm (Gate 3).}
Mean victim $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ is negative and directionally consistent across all seeds for all three policies.
At $f{=}0.4$: \LOCAL $-7.9$\,pp, \CLUSTER $-4.9$\,pp (unchanged from $f{=}0.2$), \GLOBAL $-6.1$\,pp.
Downstream harm is not proportional to $\Delta\tau$: because each policy's clean threshold sits at a different point on the victim's score distribution, the same displacement traverses different amounts of probability mass, so \GLOBAL's smaller shift can still produce a larger TPR loss.
Absolute clean$\to$poisoned victim TPR at $f{=}0.4$ (Table~\ref{tab:baseline} clean column): \GLOBAL $0.793\to0.732$, \LOCAL $0.777\to0.698$, \CLUSTER $0.742\to0.693$.
Under \GLOBAL, all eight non-victims receive the identical $\Delta\tau$ (Table~\ref{tab:spillover}), but their TPR was not recomputed, so non-victim impact is inferred, not measured.
\LOCAL/\CLUSTER non-victim threshold propagation is bounded by construction.

\subsection{Lowering Attack: Secondary Results (Conditional)}

Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold-shift}(b) shows threshold shift for Low-Score-Benign + Threshold-Lower.
The adversary's goal is to increase false-alarm burden; the $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}{>}0$ below is a threshold-displacement indicator, not a security benefit.

\textbf{Local and Cluster succeed; Global is marginal.}
\LOCAL achieves $\Delta\tau = -0.164$ $[-0.176, -0.154]$ at $f{=}0.4$ with elevated FPR disparity ($\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)} = +0.053$); Gate-3 $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = +11.8$\,pp is consistent with threshold displacement.
\CLUSTER similarly achieves $-0.154$ $[-0.167, -0.142]$ at $f{=}0.4$ with $\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)} = +0.060$ and $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = +10.3$\,pp.
\GLOBAL is structurally bounded: at $f{=}0.4$, $\Delta\tau = -0.018$ $[-0.020, -0.017]$, Gate 1 passes only at $f{\ge}0.2$, and $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = +2.1$\,pp.

\textbf{FPR disparity (operational harm).}
The operational harm metric is $\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)}$ (dimensionless): $+0.053$ (\LOCAL, 10/10 seeds) and $+0.060$ (\CLUSTER, 8/10 seeds) at $f{=}0.4$, while \GLOBAL shows $-0.019$ because under a shared threshold the FPR rises proportionally across clients, which reduces relative disparity.
Under a shared threshold, $\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)}$ understates harm: the absolute FPR still rises, so the negative value reflects dispersion reduction, not reduced alarm burden.
Absolute clean$\to$poisoned FPR at $f{=}0.4$ (Table~\ref{tab:baseline} clean column): \GLOBAL $0.041\to0.043$, \LOCAL $0.043\to0.046$, \CLUSTER $0.043\to0.049$.

\textbf{Mechanism and conditionality.}
Lowering a $q{=}0.95$ quantile requires displacing scores in the upper-tail support; random position replacement achieves this with probability $\approx f$ (40\% at $f{=}0.4$), leaving 60\% of upper-tail support intact.
The \GLOBAL policy compounds this via $1/N$ dilution.
These results are presented as a lower bound under Replace-Fixed-Budget; targeted upper-tail removal would achieve larger shifts.

\subsection{Policy-Differentiation Summary}

\input{tables/table_spillover}

Table~\ref{tab:spillover} quantifies the threshold-propagation contrast under the raising attack at $f{=}0.4$.
\GLOBAL propagates the threshold shift to all nine clients: the non-victim $\Delta\tau$ equals the victim $\Delta\tau$ by construction (shared threshold), and the blast count is 8/8 non-victims in nearly every seed.
\CLUSTER produces near-\LOCAL victim-level threshold shifts ($+0.899$ vs.\ $+0.902$), though victim $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ remains smaller ($-4.9$ vs.\ $-7.9$\,pp); non-victim threshold changes are small in magnitude (mean $\Delta\tau{\approx}0.0003$, std\,$=0.017$, driven by $k$-means cluster reassignment rather than aggregation), with 6/8 non-victims showing Gate-1-material but small-magnitude threshold perturbations.
The blast count measures threshold materiality, not downstream harm: non-victim rates were not recomputed for \CLUSTER. Its mean non-victim $\Delta\tau$ is two to three orders of magnitude below the weakest victim shifts ($f{=}0.1$: $+0.033$ \GLOBAL, $+0.253$ \CLUSTER, $+0.298$ \LOCAL; Table~\ref{tab:main-results}), so large non-victim $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ changes are implausible but unverified. \GLOBAL, by contrast, transmits $\Delta\tau$ to every non-victim, though its downstream effect is likewise inferred rather than measured.
\LOCAL yields the highest per-victim shift with zero non-victim blast by policy design.
