\subsection{Clean-Baseline Policy Profile}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} reports clean-baseline dispersion across all ten seeds; lower CV(FPR) means more uniform per-client false-positive treatment, and \LOCAL is lowest ($0.333 \pm 0.034$), \GLOBAL highest ($0.905 \pm 0.039$), \CLUSTER intermediate ($0.615 \pm 0.143$) with cluster-assignment sensitivity.
Pairwise gaps, taken as within-seed paired differences, are positive with bootstrap CIs excluding zero: G--L $0.572$ $[0.537, 0.607]$, G--C $0.291$ $[0.214, 0.358]$, C--L $0.281$ $[0.201, 0.374]$ (unrounded per-seed aggregates); Fig.~\ref{fig:clean-fpr} shows the per-client FPR distributions.
Lower dispersion does not imply better detection: \GLOBAL retains the best P10 Macro-F1, effectively a worst-client value with nine clients, at $0.398$ against \LOCAL's $0.335$.

\subsection{Raising Attack: Primary Results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold-shift}(a) report threshold shift and downstream detection harm for High-Score-Benign + Threshold-Raise.

\input{tables/table_main_results}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_threshold_shift.pdf}
\caption{Mean threshold shift $\Delta\tau$ vs.\ injection fraction $f$ for raising (a) and lowering (b) attacks.
Error bars are 95\% bootstrap CIs over 10 seeds; \GLOBAL is diluted by $1/N$, and Random-Benign controls are near-null (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}).
Panel (b) uses the legend of panel (a).}
\label{fig:threshold-shift}
\end{figure}

All three policies clear every gate at every non-zero fraction, with a Gate-1 pass rate of 10/10 seeds throughout, a seed passing when at least 5 of its 9 victim clients show a material, sign-correct shift.
For \LOCAL and \CLUSTER all nine clients are individually material in every seed; for \GLOBAL at $f{=}0.1$ two Provision security cameras (PT-737E and PT-838) are material in only 2/10 and 4/10 seeds respectively and one thermostat in 9/10, yet the victim-majority condition still holds because at least six of the nine are material in every seed.
Gate 2 holds as specified, and the primary and control intervals are additionally disjoint everywhere---a descriptive observation, not a test of their difference.
For the control, mean $\Delta\tau$ is two orders of magnitude smaller than the tail-injection shift at the same fraction, no seed meets the Gate-1 criterion (0/10), and $|\Delta\mathrm{TPR}|{\le}1$\,pp at every fraction and policy (Table~\ref{tab:main-results} gives representative $f{=}0.4$ rows).
That magnitude gap, rather than the control's non-significance, attributes the targeted effects to tail contamination instead of position perturbation.

Magnitude differs by policy: at $f{=}0.4$, \LOCAL and \CLUSTER give the largest shifts, $\Delta\tau = +0.902$ $[+0.864, +0.944]$ and $+0.899$ $[+0.859, +0.941]$, while \GLOBAL gives a diluted but still material $+0.100$ $[+0.096, +0.105]$.
The $1/N$ dilution is definitional, yet the residual ($\approx$10\% of the local shift) still produces measurable harm.

Mean victim $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ is negative and directionally consistent across all ten seeds for all three policies, so Gate 3 passes everywhere.
At $f{=}0.4$: \LOCAL $-7.9$\,pp, \CLUSTER $-4.9$\,pp (equal to the $f{=}0.2$ point estimate to one decimal; no interval on that difference is estimated, so the apparent saturation is not separated from seed-level variation), \GLOBAL $-6.1$\,pp.
Downstream harm need not track $\Delta\tau$ monotonically, consistent with each policy's clean threshold sitting at a different point of the victim's score distribution, which was not characterized here, so this is an interpretation, not a measured result.

\subsection{Lowering Attack: Secondary Results (Conditional)}

Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold-shift}(b) shows threshold shift for Low-Score-Benign + Threshold-Lower, whose goal is to increase false-alarm burden; the $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}{>}0$ below is a threshold-displacement indicator, not a security benefit.
At $f{=}0.4$, \LOCAL reaches $\Delta\tau = -0.164$ $[-0.176, -0.154]$ with $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = +11.8$\,pp.
\CLUSTER reaches $-0.154$ $[-0.167, -0.142]$ with $+10.3$\,pp.
\GLOBAL is structurally bounded: $\Delta\tau = -0.018$ $[-0.020, -0.017]$, with $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = +2.1$\,pp, and its Gate 1 passes only at $f{\ge}0.2$.

The disparity metric $\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)}$ (dimensionless) is $+0.053$ for \LOCAL (10/10 seeds) and $+0.060$ for \CLUSTER (8/10 seeds, meeting the consistency requirement at its boundary), while \GLOBAL shows $-0.019$.
Under a shared threshold every client's FPR moves in the same direction, which can reduce the coefficient of variation even as absolute FPR rises; that negative value reflects dispersion reduction, not reduced alarm burden.
Absolute clean$\to$poisoned FPR at $f{=}0.4$: \GLOBAL $0.041\to0.043$, \LOCAL $0.043\to0.046$, \CLUSTER $0.043\to0.049$.
Lowering a $q{=}0.95$ quantile requires displacing scores in the upper-tail support, which random position replacement achieves with probability $\approx f$, leaving 60\% of that support intact at $f{=}0.4$; \GLOBAL compounds the effect via $1/N$ dilution, so these results are a lower bound under Replace-Fixed-Budget.

\subsection{Policy-Differentiation Summary}

\input{tables/table_spillover}

Table~\ref{tab:spillover} quantifies threshold propagation under the raising attack at $f{=}0.4$.
\GLOBAL applies the same shift to all nine clients, so non-victim $\Delta\tau$ equals victim $\Delta\tau$ by construction; the material non-victim count nonetheless falls below 8/8 in a minority of seeds, because the Gate-1 floor $\delta\tau_{j,s}$ scales with each client's calibration IQR and a shared shift can fall below that floor at the widest-spread clients.
\CLUSTER produces near-\LOCAL victim shifts ($+0.899$ vs.\ $+0.902$) with smaller victim $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ ($-4.9$ vs.\ $-7.9$\,pp), and its non-victim changes are signed and near-symmetric (mean $+0.0003$, std $0.017$, driven by $k$-means reassignment rather than aggregation), so the small mean reflects cancellation rather than negligible perturbation: 6/8 non-victims meet the Gate-1 criterion, with typical magnitudes about an order below \CLUSTER's own weakest victim shift ($+0.253$ at $f{=}0.1$).
That count measures threshold materiality, not downstream harm, and \CLUSTER non-victim rates were likewise not recomputed.
\LOCAL yields the highest per-victim shift with no material non-victim shifts at all, by policy design.
