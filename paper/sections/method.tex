\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Dataset and Federation}

We use N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot}, a labeled IoT network traffic dataset covering nine physical device types (doorbells, baby monitor, security camera, smart TV, thermostat, webcam).
Each device constitutes one federation client; all nine are eligible for calibration.
Calibration set sizes range from 2,622 (Ecobee Thermostat) to 9,909 (Danmini Doorbell) benign samples per device (all $\ge$100, satisfying the eligibility threshold).
The federation trains a per-client autoencoder on benign traffic using FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} for feature extraction, then enters the threshold-calibration stage.
The full configuration is: threshold quantile $q{=}0.95$; injection fractions $f \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$; injection rule \textsc{Replace-Fixed-Budget}; single-client attack scope; 10 paired (training + poisoning) seeds; reservoir = bottom/top 10\,\% tail of the poisoned client's benign calibration scores, sampled with replacement.

\subsection{Threshold Policies}

Three threshold-policy families are evaluated, covering the main design points in the personalization literature:

\begin{description}
  \item[Global.] \textsc{Global}: $\tau_{\mathrm{global}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \tau_i^{\mathrm{local}}$,
  where $\tau_i^{\mathrm{local}} = \mathrm{quantile}(\mathcal{C}_i, q)$.
  All clients receive the same threshold; any per-client shift is diluted by $1/N$.
  \item[Local.] \textsc{Local}: each client retains $\tau_i^{\mathrm{local}}$.
  Shifts are confined to the compromised client; no spillover.
  \item[Cluster.] \textsc{Cluster}: clients are grouped on a standard-scaled 4-feature fingerprint (mean, std, skew, $p_{95}$ of $\mathcal{C}_i$) via $k$-means++~\cite{arthur2007kmeanspp} ($K{=}3$ clusters of 3 clients each, \texttt{random\_state}$=42$; reassigned each seed).
  Within each cluster, the shared threshold is the cluster mean; the higher seed-to-seed variance in \textsc{Cluster} CV(FPR) reflects cluster-assignment variability.
  A compromised client may alter the threshold for all co-cluster members (\emph{intra-cluster spillover}); clients in other clusters are unaffected.
\end{description}

\subsection{Attack Parameterization}

For each (policy, attack objective, injection fraction $f \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$) combination, we run 10 paired seeds: each seed selects a training configuration and an independent poisoning draw.
A clean copy of the calibration array is preserved; only the poisoned copy is used for threshold computation.
The four source-objective pairs are:
(1)~\textsc{High-Score-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Raise} (primary raising attack),
(2)~\textsc{Low-Score-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Lower} (primary lowering attack),
(3)~\textsc{Random-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Raise} (objective-matched negative control for the raising attack), and
(4)~\textsc{Random-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Lower} (objective-matched negative control for the lowering attack).
The attack is applied to each of the nine clients as victim in turn (single-client scope), yielding 4~source-objective pairs $\times$ 3~threshold policies $\times$ 4~injection fractions $\times$ 9~victim clients $\times$ 10~seeds $= 4{,}320$ experimental cells total.
Training seed $i \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$ is paired with poisoning seed $i{+}100$ and analysis seed $i{+}300$, giving statistically independent stochasticity across training, calibration sampling, and bootstrap resampling.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

\textbf{Gate 1 (material shift, primary gate).}
Threshold shift $\Delta\tau_v = \tau_v^{\mathrm{poisoned}} - \tau_v^{\mathrm{clean}}$ is computed per seed.
A seed is \emph{Gate-1 significant} when $|\Delta\tau_v| > \delta\tau_v$ and the sign is consistent with the attack objective, where the materiality threshold is
$\delta\tau_v = \max\!\bigl(0.1 \times \mathrm{IQR}(\mathcal{C}_v^{\mathrm{clean}}),\;0.01 \times \mathrm{median}_j(\mathrm{IQR}(\mathcal{C}_j^{\mathrm{clean}}))\bigr)$.
Gate-1 passes when $\ge 8/10$ seeds are significant and the victim-majority condition ($\ge 5/9$ victims significant, i.e.\ a simple majority of the nine federation clients) holds.

\textbf{Gate 2 (directional excess over control).}
Bootstrap 95\% CI on mean $\Delta\tau$ excludes zero for the primary strategy but spans zero for the negative control (\textsc{Random-Benign}).

\textbf{Gate 3 (downstream harm).}
Mean victim TPR change, $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$, is directionally consistent (negative for raising, positive for lowering) across $\ge 8/10$ seeds.

\textbf{Confidence intervals.}
All CIs are 95\% percentile bootstrap over 10 seed-level aggregates, with $B{=}10{,}000$ resamples.
Percentile bootstrap coverage at $n{=}10$ may deviate from nominal at the tails; reported CIs should be interpreted as approximate.
AUROC invariance is verified as a protocol check: since test scores and labels are unchanged, AUROC must be identical under poisoning; any deviation would indicate a protocol violation.

\textbf{Gate constant justification.}
The 10\,\% IQR coefficient for Gate-1 materiality ($0.1{\times}\mathrm{IQR}$) was set to exceed calibration natural instability observed in the clean baseline while remaining smaller than the expected tail-injection shift at $f{=}0.1$.
The 1\,\% cross-client floor ($0.01{\times}\mathrm{median}_j(\mathrm{IQR})$) prevents degenerate clients with near-zero score spread from counting as material on arbitrarily small shifts.
Both constants were locked from clean-artifact statistics before any poisoned run was executed.

\subsection{Isolation Sanity Check}

To confirm that the attack is confined to the calibration stage, we verify: (i)~AUROC is invariant under poisoning for all cells, (ii)~per-client rank ordering of clean anomaly scores is unchanged, and (iii)~model weights before and after calibration are bitwise identical.
All three hold in all 4320 cells.
