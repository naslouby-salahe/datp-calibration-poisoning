\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Dataset and Federation}

The evaluation uses N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot}, a labeled IoT network traffic dataset covering nine physical devices spanning five device types (doorbell, baby monitor, security camera, thermostat, webcam).
Each device constitutes one federation client; all nine are eligible for calibration.
Calibration set sizes range from 2,622 (Ecobee Thermostat) to 35,048 (Philips Baby Monitor) benign samples per device (all $\ge$100, satisfying the eligibility threshold).
Each device's benign stream is partitioned chronologically into a $60\%$ train / $1\%$ gap / $20\%$ calibration / $1\%$ gap / $18\%$ test split; the two gaps avoid temporal leakage.
Training and calibration use benign traffic only, and the attack never touches test data, test labels, or training labels.
Attack-labeled records are loaded from the N-BaIoT attack folders, scaled with the device scaler fitted on training data, scored as the \texttt{test\_attack} split, and used only as the positive class for TPR evaluation; they never enter FedAvg training, benign calibration arrays, poisoning reservoirs, threshold recomputation, or injected buffers.
The federation trains a per-client autoencoder on benign traffic using FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} for feature extraction, then enters the threshold-calibration stage at a fixed quantile $q{=}0.95$.
Table~\ref{tab:repro} lists the autoencoder architecture, training hyperparameters, and the calibration size range; injection fractions, seeds, and the reservoir are detailed under Attack Parameterization below.

\subsection{Threshold Policies}

Three threshold-policy families are evaluated, covering the main design points in the personalization literature:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textsc{Global}.]
  \[
    \tau_i^{\mathrm{local}} = \operatorname{quantile}(\mathcal{C}_i,q),
    \qquad
    \tau^{\mathrm{global}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau_i^{\mathrm{local}} .
  \]
  Global assigns the same $\tau^{\mathrm{global}}$ to every client; a single poisoned local threshold contributes only $1/N$ of the shared threshold shift.

  \item[\textsc{Local}.] Each client retains $\tau_i = \tau_i^{\mathrm{local}}$.
  Shifts are confined to the compromised client; no cross-client threshold propagation.

  \item[\textsc{Cluster}.]
  Clients are grouped on a standard-scaled 4-feature fingerprint (mean, std, skew, $p_{95}$ of $\mathcal{C}_i$) via $k$-means++~\cite{arthur2007kmeanspp} ($K{=}3$, \texttt{random\_state}$=42$; clustering recomputed after each attack draw so a poisoned fingerprint can change the compromised client's cluster assignment).
  Within each cluster, the shared threshold is the cluster mean; the higher seed-to-seed variance in \textsc{Cluster} CV(FPR) reflects this cluster-assignment sensitivity.
  A compromised client may perturb the threshold for co-cluster members (\emph{intra-cluster threshold perturbation}); clients in other clusters are unaffected.
\end{description}

\subsection{Attack Parameterization}

For each (policy, attack objective, injection fraction $f \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$) combination, the protocol runs 10 paired seeds: each seed fixes a training configuration and an independent poisoning draw.
A clean copy of the calibration array is preserved; only the poisoned copy is used for threshold computation.
The four source-objective pairs are:
(1)~\textsc{High-Score-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Raise} (primary raising attack),
(2)~\textsc{Low-Score-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Lower} (primary lowering attack),
(3)~\textsc{Random-Benign\,+\,Threshold-Raise} (objective-matched negative control for the raising attack), and
(4)~\textsc{Random-Benign} paired with \textsc{Threshold-Lower} (negative control for the lowering attack).
The attack is applied to each of the nine clients as victim in turn (single-client scope), yielding 4~source-objective pairs $\times$ 3~threshold policies $\times$ 4~injection fractions $\times$ 9~victim clients $\times$ 10~seeds $= 4{,}320$ experimental cells total.
Training seed $i \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$ is paired with poisoning seed $i{+}100$ and analysis seed $i{+}300$, separating training, calibration sampling, and bootstrap resampling stochasticity.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

\textbf{Gate 1 (material shift, primary gate).}
Threshold shift $\Delta\tau_v = \tau_v^{\mathrm{poisoned}} - \tau_v^{\mathrm{clean}}$ is computed per seed.
A seed is \emph{Gate-1 significant} when $|\Delta\tau_v| > \delta\tau_v$ and the sign is consistent with the attack objective, where the materiality threshold is
$\delta\tau_v = \max\!\bigl(0.1 \times \mathrm{IQR}(\mathcal{C}_v^{\mathrm{clean}}),\;0.01 \times \mathrm{median}_j(\mathrm{IQR}(\mathcal{C}_j^{\mathrm{clean}}))\bigr)$.
Gate-1 passes when $\ge 8/10$ seeds are significant and the victim-majority condition ($\ge$5/9 of the nine clients, each taken as victim in turn, are significant) holds.

\textbf{Gate 2 (directional excess over control).}
Bootstrap 95\% CI on mean $\Delta\tau$ excludes zero for the primary strategy but spans zero for the negative control (\textsc{Random-Benign}).

\textbf{Gate 3 (downstream harm).}
Mean victim TPR change, $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$, is directionally consistent (negative for raising, positive for lowering) across $\ge 8/10$ seeds.

\textbf{Confidence intervals.}
All CIs are 95\% percentile bootstrap over 10 seed-level aggregates, with $B{=}10{,}000$ resamples.
Primary claim gates are evaluated over these paired seed-level aggregates; the per-victim-seed cells are descriptive support, not independent units.
With ten paired seeds, percentile-bootstrap intervals are approximate uncertainty estimates rather than exact coverage guarantees, and may deviate from nominal at the tails.
A two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on ten seed-level mean victim $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}$ values (raising, $f{=}0.4$) gives $W{=}0$, $p{=}0.002$ (exact) for all three policies.

\textbf{Gate constant justification.}
The 10\,\% IQR coefficient for Gate-1 materiality ($0.1{\times}\mathrm{IQR}$) exceeds the calibration instability observed in the clean baseline while remaining below the expected tail-injection shift at $f{=}0.1$; the 1\,\% cross-client floor ($0.01{\times}\mathrm{median}_j(\mathrm{IQR})$) prevents degenerate clients with near-zero score spread from counting as material on arbitrarily small shifts.
Both constants were locked from clean-artifact statistics before any poisoned run was executed.

\subsection{Isolation Sanity Check}

To confirm that the attack is confined to the calibration stage, the protocol verifies: (i)~AUROC is invariant under poisoning for all cells, (ii)~per-client rank ordering of clean anomaly scores is unchanged, and (iii)~model weights before and after calibration are bitwise identical.
All three hold in all 4320 cells.
