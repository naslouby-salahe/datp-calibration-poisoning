\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Score-level proxy and detectability.}
The experiment operates at the score level: reconstruction scores in the calibration buffer are replaced programmatically rather than through generated traffic.
A complete end-to-end attack would additionally require generating network packets whose autoencoder reconstruction error lands in the target tail of the victim's score distribution---a non-trivial requirement spanning feature engineering, the encoder-decoder nonlinearity, and physical network behavior.
We present this work as a \emph{vulnerability-surface characterization}, not a deployed attack; traffic-level realizability is future work.
A related implementation limitation: with-replacement sampling from the tail reservoir at $f{=}0.4$ produces duplicate scores in the calibration buffer.
With reservoir size $R{\approx}10\,\%{\cdot}N$ and the smallest calibration set ($N{\approx}2{,}622$), the expected collision count $(N{\cdot}f)^2/(2R){\approx}2{,}100$ expected collision pairs at $f{=}0.4$---easily flagged by a uniqueness check; evading requires score perturbation at the cost of leaving the exact tail boundary.
We flag detectability as an open problem.

\textbf{GLOBAL spillover, lowering bounds, and policy implications.}
The $1/N$-diluted shift is a definitional property of the \textsc{Global} policy; what is empirical is that the residual magnitude ($\approx$10\,\% of the local shift, $\Delta\mathrm{TPR}{=}{-}6.1$\,pp at $f{=}0.4$) remains measurable above the seed-to-seed variance.
The lowering attack's conditionality stems from the percentile mechanism: random position replacement displaces upper-tail support only with probability $\approx f$, leaving 60\,\% of the upper tail intact at $f{=}0.4$.
The Local/Cluster lowering results ($\Delta\mathrm{CV(FPR)} {=} {+}0.05$--$0.06$ disparity increase, $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} {=} {+}10$--$12$\,pp detection-rate shift) are therefore a \emph{lower bound} under the \textsc{Replace-Fixed-Budget} mechanism; adversaries with access to the buffer's rank structure could achieve larger threshold displacements and correspondingly larger alarm-burden effects.
These results together reveal a structural trade-off in policy design: \textsc{Local} maximizes per-client isolation but maximizes per-victim exposure; \textsc{Global} limits per-victim shift via averaging but propagates residual harm federation-wide.
Defense designers must choose which risk to bound.

\textbf{Scope limitations.}
Claims are bounded to N-BaIoT (9 clients, autoencoder, FedAvg, $q{=}0.95$, calibration sets of $\geq$100 to $\approx$9900 benign samples per device).
Generalizability to other anomaly detectors (isolation forest, LOF, VAE), FL algorithms (FedProx, SCAFFOLD), federation sizes, and IoT datasets is not established and is future work.
The $n{=}10$ seed bootstrap CI coverage may differ from nominal at the tails.
This paper does not propose or evaluate defenses.
Any effective defense must address the calibration buffer as an unmonitored input channel, validate submitted thresholds against server-side priors, or impose calibration-set integrity constraints --- requirements that differ from training-phase defense requirements and merit independent study.
